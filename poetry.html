Leaving the Amaltas

A debut collection, in the making.

On the 17th of March, there will be an afternoon,
where the Amaltas will shrug-
of their heavy winter coats-
too forcefully,
and tumble headlong into spring,
as if by innocent mistake.
And I will catch them.
And tuck them gently behind the ear of my grandmother,
So they can still stay warm.
She will squeal in delight,
And ask me if I'd like Amaltas embroidered on the corners, of the quilt she was knitting.
I'd tut and tell her it's too much work.
On the 17th of July, there will be a day,
where the rains will drive away the clouds,
and replace the sky with a grey watercolour wash.
And the Amaltas will be the only colour for miles.
I will brush the dirt off its hanging buds,
and run its butter-like-petals through my fingertips.
And we'll both complain about the rain, to my grandmother - still knitting.
And she will smile and apologize
and say it's her fault,
for she spun the clouds into yarn for her quilt.
On the 17th of August The Sun's heart will tire of separation,
and it will sneak out unnoticed from behind the clouds,
To meet me below the Amaltas.
And I will spend long afternoon hours,
gazing at the sunlight-draped-Amaltas,
committing every crevice of its countenance to memory.
By-hearting it's shades-
Its viridans, chartreuses and dulled limes.
And I will lean back against my grandmother's knee, her quilt now flowing off her lap-
And I will marvel,
at how grass and sunshine and Amaltas,
could make our little concrete compound,
seem infinite.
She will smile wistfully,
And spin the sunshine into her quilt for good measure.
On the 17th of September I will leave. -the Amaltas
-hanging.
To catch themselves when they fall,
out of winter.
I wonder if the next time I see it all
it will all look smaller than I remember.
If I would have grown too big to appreciate the small things.
Or if the Amaltas would have grown too tall, For me to wrap my arms around them.
But my grandmother will come around,
And place a finished quilt-
-with Amaltas embroidered around the corners, On my suitcase.
And my brother will wrap his arms around me
and I will run my fingertips through his hair, that is really no different than Amalta petals.
And I will realise -
that some things - like arms and quilts
Will always fit around you.
* * * * *
To Harith and Nani
Amaltas are a native Indian flowering tree, nicknamed the Indian Laburnum.
